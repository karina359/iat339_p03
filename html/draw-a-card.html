Strange Sounds and Beyond

Microsite for Strange Sounds and Beyond

Timeline

5 weeks

Team

Angelique, Amy, Clara, Chantal

Role

Design Manager

Tools

Figma

Objective

Showcasing Strange Sounds from Beyond

A redesigned website for the Strange Sounds From Beyond, an Amsterdam-based festival and platform for cross-spectrum music for Foundations of Designing Interactions IAT 235.

Inspiration

Looking at Contemporary Designers

During this project we were encouraged to study contemporary graphic designers such as David Carson, Chris Ashworth, and Experimental Jetset.

Research

How do we develop a Art Direction?

Our team started off by researching design qualities of our assigned designer David Carson as well as Lipton principles to guide our compositions. We then worked on posters and graphics to figure out what design language worked for our client.

Week 1

Initial Explorations

We started off strongly inspired by designer David Carson, working with his visual ideas of image and text collage and distrusted type.

Week 2

Expanded Experiments

Pivoting to more image-driven designs, we used high-contrast colours, textures, and distortions to create a layered focal point in the poster.

Week 3

Refining Style

To clarify and ground one true anchor point in our designs, we played with contrasting hues and saturations, as well as adding lines.

Week 4

Another Domain

Moving from a poster format to microsites, we explored shape and hue as methods to emphasize a radial focal point.

Final

Introducing Strange Sounds from Beyond

Our final microsite combines exploration of shape and texture as methods of grounding a main focal point. In addition, pops of contrasting colour are used to indicate moments of interaction for the user.

Key Decisions

Spotlight the Artists

Showcasing the artist lineup was our top priority, and this informed our design and information hierarchy of our site.

In our designs the artist is highlighted through using bold and colourful shapes, with use of overlap to cause tension and bring the viewer's eyes through the selection of artists.

Key Decisions

Contrasting Hues Indicate Interactions

Key areas of interest for the viewer are indicated by vibrant, contrasting colours, which pop out brightly compared to the greyscale frame of the microsite. This creates a fun and playful moment of interaction, and also draws the viewer's attention to buttons and images. As such, these pops of colour are used sparingly throughout the microsite.

Art direction

Colours and Skews

Visual elements and text are slightly skewed to create more visual interest, while not sacrificing readability.

The use of bright, overlapping colours amongst a mostly greyscale context is reminiscent of grunge and punk aesthetic. These pops of colours are used on interactable elements such as buttons.

Art direction

Distorted Type

Type is distorted through methods such as skews and overlaps. A collage like effect is achieved through the combination of multiple typefaces within a single cluster.

User Experience

Website Flow

Users navigate through the various subpages by clicking buttons on the landing page.
